Chapter 1

The First Consultation: What You Are Really Trying to Build

The First Consultation

The first consultation is not simply an opportunity to hear the client's story. It is the beginning of an investigation.

Practitioner's principle:
Investigate the story. Do not decide the case too early.

The Case Map

The investigation should begin by trying to build a coherent map:

  • Patient
  • Pre-treatment condition
  • Medical event
  • Treatment
  • Alleged problem
  • Injury
  • Subsequent treatment
  • Current condition
  • Loss

Around this central sequence sit the records, witnesses, healthcare providers, potential defendants, potential experts, dates and unanswered questions.

Why Dates Matter

In a medical-negligence case, time may become relevant to:

  • the medical chronology
  • the sequence of clinical decisions
  • the development of the injury
  • causation
  • prescription
  • statutory notice
  • procedural steps
  • expert opinions
The medical chronology

The chronological sequence of the patient's medical history, treatment, consultations, investigations and relevant events.

The chronology helps the practitioner identify what happened, when it happened and what information remains missing.

The sequence of clinical decisions

The sequence in which healthcare decisions were made during the patient's treatment.

The purpose is to understand what was known or observable at each stage and what clinical decision followed.

The development of the injury

The progression of the patient's condition through the relevant medical events and subsequent treatment.

Causation

The investigation of the connection between the alleged medical event or departure from proper care and the injury complained of.

Prescription

A time-related legal issue concerning whether a claim may still be instituted within the applicable limitation period.

Statutory notice

A potentially important procedural requirement where legislation requires notice before legal proceedings are instituted against certain entities.

Procedural steps

The sequence of steps required to investigate, prepare and potentially institute the claim.

Expert opinions

Specialist opinions used to address questions that require specialised knowledge, including questions concerning clinical standards, departure and causation.

The Practitioner's Test

Before deciding whether a case has merit, the practitioner should continue investigating the relevant questions:

  • What was the applicable standard of care?
  • Was there a departure from that standard?
  • Is there a question of wrongfulness?
  • Is there a question of negligence?
  • Can causation be established?
  • What loss is alleged?
Closing principle:
The first consultation should produce a structured investigation, not a premature conclusion.
