Before You Sue

Preliminary Investigation, Viability and Pre-Litigation Strategy

The Starting Point

A medical negligence case should not begin with a summons. It should begin with a disciplined investigation.

Before litigation is considered, the practitioner must determine what actually happened, what evidence exists, what remains uncertain, who may be responsible, and whether the claim is legally and factually capable of being pursued.

Practitioner's Principle

Do not sue because the story sounds wrong. Sue when the investigation shows that the case can properly be built.

What This Book Is About

Book 1 began with the first consultation and the initial investigation of the patient's story.

Book 2 takes the investigation further. It asks whether the available information is sufficient to move from an initial complaint toward a properly formulated claim.

The practitioner must test the facts before committing the client to litigation.

The Question Behind Every Chapter

The central question is not simply: “Can we sue?”

The better question is: “Have we investigated enough to know whether we should?”

The objective is not to manufacture a case. The objective is to discover whether a case exists.

Investigate the story before deciding the case.
