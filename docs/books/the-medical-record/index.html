The Medical Record

Reading, reconstructing and interrogating the clinical record

Practitioner Principle

The medical record is not merely a collection of documents. It is the evidentiary story of what was known, what was done, what was not done, and when those things occurred.

The Medical Record as Evidence

In medical negligence litigation, the medical record often provides the foundation from which the clinical chronology, decision-making, standard of care and causation are investigated.

But a record should not simply be read from beginning to end. It should be interrogated.

The practitioner must learn to identify what each entry says, what it does not say, how entries relate to one another, and whether the documentary record is consistent with the patient's account and the available expert evidence.

Reading the Record in Practice

This book uses reconstructed medical records to demonstrate how a practitioner can move from documentation to analysis.

Look for:

  • What happened?
  • When did it happen?
  • Who knew about it?
  • What decision was made?
  • What action followed?
  • Was the patient monitored?
  • Was deterioration recognised?
  • Was escalation required?
  • Are there contradictions or gaps?

The Central Question

The question is not simply: “What does the record say?”

The better question is:

“What does the record allow us to prove, what does it leave unanswered, and what further evidence is required?”

Read the record. Reconstruct the events. Test the decisions.
