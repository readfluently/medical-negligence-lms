The Medical Record

Reading, Reconstructing and Interrogating the Clinical Record

The Starting Point

The medical record is often the evidentiary foundation of a medical-negligence investigation.

It records what was documented about the patient's condition, what practitioners observed, what decisions were made, what treatment was provided, and how the patient's condition changed over time.

Practitioner's Principle

Do not merely read the medical record. Reconstruct the clinical story from it, test what it reveals, and identify what it leaves unanswered.

What This Book Is About

Book 1 began with the patient's story. Book 2 tested whether the emerging claim could properly proceed.

Book 3 turns to the documentary evidence that may confirm, contradict or complicate that story.

The practitioner will learn how to read different parts of the medical record, reconstruct the chronology, identify gaps and inconsistencies, and connect the documentary evidence to the questions of negligence and causation.

The book will also use reconstructed medical records to demonstrate how these documents should be interrogated in practice.

The Central Question

The question is not simply: “What does the medical record say?”

The better question is:

“What does the record allow us to establish, what does it leave unanswered, and what further evidence is required?”

Read the record. Reconstruct the events. Test the decisions.
