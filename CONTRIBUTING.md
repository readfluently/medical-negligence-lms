# Contributing to the Practitioner-to-Practitioner Medical Negligence LMS

Thank you for contributing to the Practitioner-to-Practitioner Medical Negligence LMS.

This project is intended to support professional learning for medical negligence attorneys, advocates and medical experts.

Contributions may include:

- Educational content
- Case studies
- Training exercises
- Training games
- Assessments
- Facilitator materials
- Software development
- Documentation
- Accessibility improvements
- User experience improvements

## Educational Content

When contributing educational content, contributors should:

1. Clearly identify the jurisdiction.
2. Provide the relevant source or citation.
3. Identify the publication date where applicable.
4. Identify the applicable version or effective date where applicable.
5. Check copyright and licensing requirements.
6. Avoid confidential client information.
7. Avoid identifiable patient information.
8. Avoid privileged legal material.
9. Identify any assumptions used in fictional case studies.
10. Provide a learning objective for new training activities.

## Legal and Medical Materials

Legal and medical information can change over time.

Contributors should therefore identify:

- The source of the information
- The relevant jurisdiction
- The date of the information
- Any applicable version or effective date
- The date on which the material was reviewed

Where appropriate, material should be reviewed by a suitably qualified practitioner or subject-matter expert before publication.

## Case Studies

Case studies should preferably be:

- Fictional
- Properly anonymised
- Publicly available with appropriate permission
- Or otherwise authorised for educational use

Confidential client files must never be uploaded to this repository.

## Software Contributions

Software contributions should:

- Follow the existing project structure.
- Use clear and maintainable code.
- Avoid committing passwords, API keys or other secrets.
- Include appropriate tests where practical.
- Explain significant changes in the pull request.

## Pull Requests

A pull request should explain:

1. What has been changed.
2. Why the change was necessary.
3. Who the intended users are.
4. Whether the change affects educational content.
5. Whether additional review is required.

## Content Review

Educational content may require review before it is published to learners.

The project may use the following content states:

- Draft
- Under Review
- Approved
- Published
- Archived

## Professional Standards

All contributions should support respectful, evidence-based and professionally appropriate learning.

The purpose of the platform is to encourage practitioners to learn from one another and to develop practical professional skills.
